Why crypto only, and what that actually buys you

A card payment identifies you. The processor knows your name, your bank and your billing address, and it keeps a record linking that person to a VPN subscription. It does not matter how good the provider’s no-logs policy is if a third party is holding the one record that matters, and it does not matter that the provider promises not to look.

Crypto removes that record from the picture — but only if it is done a particular way, and most providers do not do it that way. Three things to check, of any provider, including this one:

What each coin does and does not hide

This is where most pages wave their hands, so here it is plainly. These are the three the software implements — check the list above for what this server is currently able to issue an address for.

Monero (XMR) — the private one

The chain publishes neither the recipient nor the amount. Nobody reading it can see that you paid us or how much. We detect payment with a view-only wallet that can see incoming funds and cannot spend them. If payment privacy is the reason you are on this page, this is the coin.

Bitcoin (BTC) — public, but unlinked

Bitcoin’s ledger is fully public. We derive a fresh receive address for every invoice from an extended public key, so no two customers share one and the control plane holds nothing that could spend your money. What the chain still shows is that some address paid some other address. If the coins you send are traceable to an exchange account in your name, that link exists on the chain whatever we do.

Zcash (ZEC) — transparent addresses only

We accept t-addresses, which behave like Bitcoin’s. We do not accept shielded z-addresses, and we would rather say so than imply a privacy property we cannot deliver: a shielded payment cannot be watched from a public key, so we could not tell that it had arrived.

How paying works

  1. Pick a term. One, three or twelve months. No subscription, nothing stored to charge again, nothing to cancel.
  2. Get an address. We open an invoice and show a fresh address and the exact amount, with a QR code.
  3. Send it. Your account activates on confirmation. The page updates itself — closing it does not cancel the invoice, and the token from step one is all you need.
  4. That is the end of it. When the time runs out the account stops. If you want more, renew with another payment and the new days stack on whatever is left.

Common questions

Can I pay anonymously without KYC?

There is nobody here to KYC you. We never ask for a name, an email address or a document, and there is no third-party processor in the path that could. What we cannot control is where your coins came from — if you bought them on an exchange that verified your identity, that part of the trail exists before it reaches us.

What if I send the wrong amount?

Send too little and the checkout says so, names the shortfall, and keeps the same address open so you can finish. Send too much and the invoice settles; we do not keep the difference as a rule, so get in touch.

Can I get a refund in crypto?

Yes, within 30 days. Email your invoice ID and an address and we send the full amount back by hand, from the wallet it arrived in — there is no processor in the middle and so no dispute flow. Refunding a month takes that month back off the subscription; the terms spell it out.

Do you take Ethereum, Litecoin or anything else?

Ethereum is supported through a BTCPay or hosted-gateway deployment; Litecoin is not supported at all. What any given server can take depends on how it is configured, and the checkout shows the real list rather than a wish list — it reads the addresses the server can actually issue. Bitcoin is always there. If a coin is not on the checkout, we cannot take it today, whatever this page says the software can do.
