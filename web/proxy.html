What this actually is

It is a page reader. Your browser asks us for a URL, we fetch that page from our own servers, rewrite its links so images and styles come back through us too, and hand you the result with a small banner on top. The site you are visiting sees a request from our infrastructure. It does not see your IP address, and it cannot set a cookie in your browser that it will recognise later.

And here is what it is not, because most “free proxy” pages will not tell you and the difference matters:
  • It covers one browser tab, not your device. Everything outside this tab — your other tabs, your apps, your system updates — goes out over your normal connection, from your normal address.
  • It reads pages. It is not a tunnel. No video streaming, no logging in to things, no downloads, no torrents. It fetches documents and images over ordinary web ports and refuses everything else.
  • It is not encryption between you and the site. The hop from us to them is theirs; the hop from you to us is ours. We can see what you asked for while we are fetching it. We do not write it down, and our privacy policy says so in detail, but a proxy you cannot see inside is still a proxy you are trusting.
  • Your employer or ISP still sees that you contacted us. They cannot see which page you then read, but the connection to this domain is visible to whoever carries it.

Common questions

Is this really free?

Yes, and there is no account to make. It exists because it is the cheapest honest way to show you we can build something that works before asking you for money. The VPN is the paid product; this is not a trial of it and does not expire.

Do you log what I read?

No. We keep a per-page view counter and nothing that ties a page to a person — no address, no session, no history. The audit pack documents what the service stores, and ships a verifier you can run against a live server rather than taking our word for it.

Why will it not load the site I want?

Most likely one of three things. The page needs you to be logged in, and we deliberately do not carry your cookies. The page is built entirely by JavaScript, which we do not run. Or the destination is not a public web address — we refuse private and internal ranges outright, so a proxy pointed at somebody’s router cannot become our problem.

Is a free proxy safe?

Depends entirely on who runs it, and that is the honest answer for every one of them including this one. A proxy sees your requests while it serves them. Most free proxies pay for themselves by injecting adverts, selling traffic data, or both. We do neither, we publish what we keep, and the code is open so the claim is checkable. Treat any proxy that will not tell you how it is funded as one that is funded by you.
